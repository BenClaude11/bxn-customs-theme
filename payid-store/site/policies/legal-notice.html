2. No guarantee of road legality

No BXN Customs product is sold as making a bike road legal. We don't guarantee that any bike fitted with our products complies with road rules, vehicle standards or registration requirements in any state or territory.

The legal status of an electric bike or motorbike depends on the whole bike. That includes its motor power, when and how the motor gives assistance, how its throttle works, whether it is registered, and where it is ridden. A single part can't change that on its own. Fitting one of our products does not make a non-compliant bike compliant.

3. Your responsibility for compliance

Before you buy, fit or ride with any of our products, you are responsible for working out whether your particular bike is legal where you ride it, with that product fitted and set up the way you use it.

The rules differ between states and territories. They also differ between public roads, paths and private land, and they change over time. Check the current rules with your state or territory road authority; in NSW, that's Transport for NSW. If you're not sure your bike is legal, don't ride it on public roads or paths until you've confirmed it.

In NSW, police and authorised officers can seize illegal e-bikes used on roads and road-related areas, and seized bikes may be destroyed.

4. Private-property and off-road use

Depending on the bike and how it is set up, a bike fitted with some of our products may not meet the requirements for public roads or paths. Where a product, or the bike it is fitted to, can operate outside road-legal requirements, it is intended only for lawful use on private property, or in off-road areas where that use is permitted, with the landowner's permission. Riding such a bike anywhere else may be illegal, may lead to the bike being seized, and may affect your insurance.

5. Speed limiters

Our speed limiters restrict how fast a bike can go. They don't change how the bike is classified under the law, and a limiter does not make an otherwise non-compliant bike legal on public roads or paths. Switching a limiter between settings, including wirelessly, does not change the bike's legal status.

In NSW, for example, Transport for NSW states that an e-bike that exceeds the legal limits is illegal even if its power or speed is restricted by software, an app or a switch, and that tampering with an e-bike may make it illegal. A bike capable of more than 500 watts of continuous power is still illegal even when software limits it to 500 watts or less. That limit drops to 250 watts from 1 March 2029, and from that date only EN 15194 certified e-bikes will be allowed on NSW roads.

Use a speed limiter to manage speed, not as a way to make a bike road legal.

6. Throttles

Whether a throttle is legal depends on the bike it is fitted to and on how and where it operates. In NSW, at the time of writing (October 2026), a road legal e-bike must have:

  • a maximum continuous rated power of 500 watts (250 watts from 1 March 2029)
  • a motor that does not provide power at speeds above 25 km/h
  • any throttle-only function, meaning the motor drives the bike without pedalling, that cuts out at 6 km/h.

A bike whose throttle can power it beyond these limits does not meet NSW e-bike requirements for public roads and paths. Other states and territories have their own rules. Check that your bike and its setup comply before riding anywhere other than private property.

Our products are not designed or sold to disable, bypass or alter safety systems. Don't use or install any product in a way that interferes with:

  • brakes or brake cut-off switches

  • any other safety feature.

If fitting a product would require any of this, don't fit it, and contact us.

8. Compatibility

Our products, including plug-and-play throttle harnesses, are made for the bikes and setups listed on each product page. "Plug-and-play" means a product is designed to connect without cutting or splicing wires on those listed setups only.

Bikes that look the same can differ in controller, voltage, display, connector type and pin layout, depending on model year, market, manufacturer updates and any previous modifications. Connecting a product to an incompatible setup can damage the product, controller, battery or wiring, and can make the bike behave unpredictably. Check your bike against the product page before you order and before you connect anything. If you're unsure, contact us first.

9. Installation and modification risks

Installing or modifying electrical parts carries real risks. The following can damage components, make the bike accelerate or behave unexpectedly, or create a fire risk, and any of these can cause injury:

  • wiring errors or reversed polarity
  • loose or incompatible connectors
  • the wrong voltage
  • an incompatible controller, battery or display.

Disconnect the battery before working on your bike, follow the supplied instructions exactly, and test the bike at low speed in a safe place before riding. If you're not confident doing the work, have a qualified e-bike or e-moto technician do it. Modifying your bike may also affect its manufacturer's warranty, its registration or your insurance.
